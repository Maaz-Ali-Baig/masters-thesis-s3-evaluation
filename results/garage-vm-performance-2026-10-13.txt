PROVEN (read from the tables, arithmetic mine)
----------------------------------------------
P1. Every recorded run finished without errors. The three runs of a configuration lie close together for most configurations. Largest spreads: PUT 100 MiB, 108.87 to
    120.94 MiB/s (11 percent of the minimum), PUT 1 MiB at concurrency 64, 99.08 to 109.43 (10 percent), GET 1 MiB at concurrency 4, 294.14 to 313.62 (7 percent).
P2. Latency and throughput agree with the concurrency (Little's law, concurrency divided by average latency):
    PUT 1 MiB concurrency 1: 1 MiB / 53.1, 51.1, 53.7 ms = 18.8, 19.6, 18.6 MiB/s against 18.13, 19.16, 19.10;
    PUT 1 MiB concurrency 64: 64 / 613.0, 574.8, 687.5 ms = 104.4, 111.3, 93.1 against 103.03, 109.43, 99.08;
    GET 100 MiB concurrency 8: 800 MiB / 0.9537, 0.9465, 0.9680 s = 838.9, 845.2, 826.4 against 822.94, 832.83, 835.48;
    GET 1 MiB concurrency 64: 64 / 79.2, 76.2, 79.8 ms = 808, 840, 802 against 822.37, 848.71, 800.47;
    PUT 50 KiB concurrency 8: 8 / 25.1 ms = 318.7 objects/s against 310.86.
    The values agree within 6 percent, so the tool measures what it says.
P3. PUT is slower than GET in every configuration, at the median: 7.2 times at 100 MiB (832.83 against 115.61), 6.0 times at 16 MiB, 7.2 times at 1 MiB and concurrency 8, 2.5 times at
    1 MiB and concurrency 1, 4.4 at concurrency 4, 7.7 at concurrency 16, 8.0 at concurrency 64, and 3.9 times at 50 KiB (59.01 against 14.98 MiB/s; 1208.56 against 306.83 objects per second).
P4. Average latency of one request: PUT 1 MiB at concurrency 1 about 52 ms (51.1 to 53.7), GET 1 MiB at concurrency 1 about 21 ms (19.9 to 21.3); at 50 KiB and concurrency 8: PUT 25.1 to
    26.4 ms, GET 6.6 to 6.9 ms (p99 48.4 to 50.8 ms). GET 100 MiB at concurrency 8: 946.5 to 968.0 ms, PUT 100 MiB: 6097.3 to 7045.7 ms.
P5. PUT throughput at 1 MiB grows with concurrency from 19.10 (1) to 67.50 (4), 88.27 (8) and 104.25 (16) MiB/s and does not grow above 16 (103.03 at 64, a change of -1.2 percent, the
    ranges 98.20 to 104.50 and 99.08 to 109.43 overlap). GET grows from 47.97 (1) to 297.15 (4), 636.58 (8) and 800.17 (16) and is 822.37 at 64 (+2.8 percent, ranges overlap).
P6. At 16 MiB and 100 MiB PUT is 128.04 and 115.61 MiB/s at concurrency 8, which is 23 and 11 percent above PUT 1 MiB at concurrency 16 (104.25); the 100 MiB PUT is 10 percent below the 16 MiB PUT.
    GET at 16 MiB and 100 MiB is 769.85 and 832.83, near GET 1 MiB at concurrency 16 and 64 (800.17, 822.37).
P7. The wall time of a run is far longer than its measured 60 s for small objects: the PUT 50 KiB runs lasted about 10 minutes each (Warp process, from runs.tsv: 630 s for the warm up and
    605 s for the first recorded run), while Warp reported 57 s of measurement. The extra time is outside the measured window; what the process does in it was not examined (most likely
    Warp deleting its objects after the run; not checked). The same applies, less strongly, to the other PUT runs (sar window 75 to 265 s, resource record).

SPACE (observations about Garage disk space, made while running the series)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Garage gave the space of deleted objects back late. First attempt: after the cleaning of the first PUT configuration the disks were at 57 percent for the 10 minutes the script waited; 20 minutes
later (21:54) they were at 23 to 27 percent on all three nodes, the resync queue was 0 and "garage worker list" showed all workers idle. The second series: 20 waiting episodes in the outer
log (first reading of an episode between 51 and 62 percent used), 116 lines "space: ... waiting 30 s" in total, the longest episode 28 waits (14 minutes, more than the old limit of 20 waits, so at least
three episodes would have stopped the series with the old script); no episode hit the new limit of 120 waits. Why Garage frees the space late is NOT examined (candidate: the block store removes
the files of deleted blocks only after a delay; not checked in the source or the documentation).
After the series (14 October 08:26, read on ceph0): bucket perfbench 0 objects, 0 bytes, no unfinished uploads, S3 listing 0 lines; data directories of Garage on ceph0 60 MB and 49 MB, 103 block entries left;
resync queue 0, no resync errors, no busy worker. The metadata store (LMDB) is 1.1 GB on ceph0 (it was 336 MB on 13 October 21:54) and "garage stats" still lists 409794 object, 412262 version and 513283 block_ref
entries although the bucket is empty (not examined: probably deletion markers that Garage keeps for some time). The df used space of the data disks (5.2 G and 3.2 G on ceph0) is larger than the Garage
directories, the rest was not examined (it is probably the data of the stopped systems on the same disks, not checked).

NOT PROVEN, hypotheses with the test that settles them (not to be written as findings)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H1. The fixed cost per PUT (52 ms for 1 MiB at concurrency 1, against 21 ms for GET) comes from writing the block and the metadata to several nodes before the acknowledgement. Settles: the
    Garage documentation or source for the write path (quorum, fsync default) and the planned fault tolerance run S1 (is a write acknowledged with one node down?).
H2. The GET results are partly served from the page cache. The resource record supports this for GET 1 MiB from concurrency 4 and for 50 KiB (drives delivered 14 to 36 percent and 15 percent of the client volume).
H3. The PUT plateau of about 100 to 128 MiB/s (1 MiB from 16 users, 16 MiB and 100 MiB at 8) has one common cause. By mean CPU no node is saturated in the 1 MiB PUT runs (resource record, highest
    47 percent on ceph2), for 16 MiB and 100 MiB ceph2 is at 69 to 78 percent. Settles: sar -P ALL for the cores, a run with the load generator on a fifth VM (not available).
H4. The three copies are written before the acknowledgement of a PUT. Settles: fault tolerance S1 and S2 (planned).

LIMITS OF THIS SERIES (to be repeated in the thesis)
----------------------------------------------------
L1. The load node ceph2 is also a storage node (decision of 10 October). The client CPU cost falls on ceph2, and part of the GET data of ceph2 is read locally and does not pass its network card
    (resource record).
L2. Redundancy differs between the systems (Garage three copies, SeaweedFS three copies by replication 002, RustFS erasure coding 3+3, Ceph as it will be configured), so write costs differ by design. This
    record contains no comparison between systems and no ranking.
L3. Three runs per configuration: ranges only, no statistical test. One cluster, one series, three and a half hours on one night.
L4. The waiting for free space (116 waits of 30 s in 20 episodes, a first aborted attempt) changed the timing between runs; its effect on the results was not measured. The bucket was not listed before
    the second start: at 21:54 the Garage data directories held 4.2 G and 4.1 G per node (8295 blocks), probably the GET 1 MiB dataset of the first attempt, which the script empties after the first
    configuration; the effect on the first configuration (PUT 50 KiB) was not examined. The bucket was empty after the series.
L5. Control measurements (read from the screen, no hash). BEFORE the series (13 October 20:29, in the first attempt's controls run /root/perf-garage-20261013-202931): dd of 2 GiB with direct writes and fsync on
    disk1 of each node 963 MB/s (ceph0), 1.2 GB/s (ceph1), 645 MB/s (ceph2); the network part of that log was not read. AFTER the series (14 October 08:23, output /root/perf-garage-20261014-082309, archive 1147 bytes, sha256
    969f001ebf9407d7296084eaf3f5086900f95f09d10bd567f3d51c2dc25046f6): dd 1.1 GB/s (ceph0), 648 MB/s (ceph1), 1.0 GB/s (ceph2); iperf3 from ceph2 to ceph0 8.97 Gbit/s sender and 8.96 receiver (5519 retransmits,
    as shown on the screen); iperf3 is not installed on ceph1, so the network to ceph1 was not measured. The single dd run of the same node differs between the two measurements by up to a factor of 1.9 (ceph1 1.2 GB/s
    then 648 MB/s, ceph2 645 MB/s then 1.0 GB/s), and earlier systems showed 662 MB/s, 1.1 GB/s and 905 MB/s (SeaweedFS, 12 October) and about 1.6, 1.4 and 2.8 GB/s (RustFS, 10 October, my arithmetic from 2 GiB in 1.34, 1.54 and 0.77 s). So a
    single dd run does not give a stable disk speed of the VMs; that variation is a limit for every comparison between systems.
L6. The laptop results for Garage (earlier version of the setup, one node, laptop) are not comparable with this series.
L7. The series crossed midnight; the script perf-sar.sh used here is the fixed one (commit 4ba7831). The Warp tables do not use the sar windows and are not affected.
L8. Garage data_fsync and the consistency mode were not set in garage.toml (defaults of v1.0.0, whose values were not looked up here), so the durability of an acknowledged write is not stated by this record.
