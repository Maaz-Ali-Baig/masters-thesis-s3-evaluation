OPEN QUESTIONS (hypotheses, with the test that settles them; not findings)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1. Why do the drives write 3.4 to 7.5 times the client volume in the window of a PUT run (S1) where three copies would be 3.0? Candidates: (a) metadata writes of the three LMDB stores (object, version and block_ref entries, one
    page write per update) and the deletes by the Warp cleanup in the same window; (b) data of earlier runs flushed from the page cache in the window; (c) the file system or the sar device rates count more than the data.
    Settles: the kernel counters of written bytes (/proc/diskstats) per disk (data disks and meta disk separately) before and after one PUT run, separately for the write phase and the cleanup, compared with the client bytes.
Q2. Why is the disk read rate over the three nodes constant at about 117 MiB/s for 1 MiB GET from concurrency 8 to 64? Settles: per run values, sar -d await and utilisation, a run with the page cache emptied during the run.
Q3. Is one core saturated in the 1 MiB runs? GET 1 MiB c16 and c64 are at 65 to 70 percent on every node while the throughput stays at 800 to 822 MiB/s. Settles: sar -P ALL (not recorded in this series).
Q4. Does every Garage gateway read the blocks of a GET from the disks of its own node (S3)? Settles: the Garage documentation or source for the read path (read quorum, local preference) and a GET through one endpoint
    with the network counters of all three nodes.

STATUS OF THE HYPOTHESES OF THE PERFORMANCE RECORD
--------------------------------------------------
H2 (GET partly from page cache): SUPPORTED for GET 1 MiB from concurrency 4 (drives delivered 36 percent of the client volume), 1 MiB from 8 (14 to 18 percent), 50 KiB (15 percent), 16 MiB and 100 MiB (43 and 39 percent); at
   one user 82 percent. So the GET figures of this series must be labelled as measurements with a large cache share.
H3 (common cause of the PUT plateau): OPEN. For 1 MiB no node is saturated by mean CPU, and iowait and network are low (S3, S4, S5); for 16 MiB and 100 MiB ceph2 is at 72 and 78 percent.
H1 and H4: not tested by these data.

LIMITS
------
L1. The load node is also a storage node (decision of 10 October); in this series it is the busiest node in 15 of 17 configurations, below 80 percent CPU everywhere.
L2. sar %util, await and queue depth are not in the table (only rates), so a drive saturation cannot be excluded or shown here. Per core CPU is not in the table (Q3).
L3. The windows are the whole Warp process, much longer than the measured minute for PUT (S7), so the PUT rates and ratios are not rates of the measured minute. The volume ratio (S1) is the better figure but still
    mixes the measured minute with the cleanup and anything the drives did later.
L4. Means of three runs per configuration; single run values are in the per run tables.
L5. The waiting for free space between runs (performance record, section SPACE) produced disk and CPU activity outside the run windows; its effect on the page cache or on the following run was not measured.
L6. The resource record for SeaweedFS used a window of about 10 percent more than the measured minute; for Garage the PUT windows are 1.3 to 10 times the measured minute (S7), so the PUT volume figures of the two records
    must not be put side by side without this limit.
