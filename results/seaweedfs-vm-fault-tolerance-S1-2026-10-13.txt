PROVEN (read from the console, the ledger and the timeline)
-----------------------------------------------------------
P1. In this run 24 writes (probe 701 to 724) were acknowledged by a surviving S3 endpoint and are not readable afterwards through any of the three endpoints (404 NoSuchKey, absent from the listing,
    absent in the filer of ceph0). The loss was already present 30 s after the first of them (the read during the outage found 701 missing). Acknowledged writes before the stop and from 725 on are readable.
P2. The file counts of the surviving nodes did not grow between 10:01:00 and 10:10:09 although 18 writes were acknowledged in that time (706 to 724), and they grew in step with the acknowledged writes
    after 10:10:09. So the 24 acknowledged writes did not produce stored files on ceph0 or on ceph2 (a read of the counts, not a proof of the mechanism).
P3. After the outage ceph1 lacks 19 files that ceph0 and ceph2 have, and the difference stays constant for 28 minutes: no repair of the missed writes was seen within the watch. All the objects of that
    period were readable through all three endpoints (they are part of the 1639 readable objects), so a read through ceph1 is served from the other two copies.
P4. The 53.7 s gap and the 14 failed attempts show that clients that are sent to the stopped node wait for a time out of about 25 to 30 s (the script uses a 25 s read time out; this is the client setting,
    not a SeaweedFS value) and that a retry through another endpoint succeeds within the same round.
P5. Reads of everything acknowledged before the stop worked during the outage through the surviving endpoint (745 of 746; the one failure is probe 701, the first write of the outage).

NOT PROVEN, open questions with the test that settles them (not to be written as findings)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1. Why were 24 writes acknowledged and not stored? Candidates: (a) the S3 gateway or filer acknowledges before the chunk write to the volume server is done or has failed (replication 002 needs three
    volume servers; with ceph1 gone the master may find no writable volume), (b) the entry is written to the filer store of one node and lost, (c) a fault of this run. Settles: repeat S1 with a capture
    of the HTTP response of one PUT per round (curl -v: status, ETag, headers) and a read of the same key directly after the acknowledgement through the same endpoint, the SeaweedFS log of the three
    containers with a log level that writes to the container log (CORRECTION of 13 October, 15:08: the container logs are not empty, they hold the S3 gateway errors of the outage window, see results/seaweedfs-vm-fault-tolerance-S1-repeat-2026-10-13.txt; the log time stamps are UTC, the first read of the log used the local time and found nothing), and the master volume layout (writable volumes) during
    the outage. Also read the SeaweedFS source for the S3 PUT path when no volume can be assigned.
Q2. Why did writes become storable again at about 10:10 (725 is readable, acknowledged 10:10:27, and the files grow from 10:10:09 on) while ceph1 was still DOWN (last DOWN row 10:10:54, start command
    finished 10:11:22)? Not examined.
Q3. Does the lag of ceph1 (19 files) disappear later, by a vacuum, by "weed shell volume.fix.replication" or by itself? Not tested beyond 28 minutes. The repair is not automatic within 28 minutes.
Q4. The listing of 1588 entries against 1594 readable probe objects (1639 readable minus 45 seed objects) is not explained (the listing was counted with "wc -l" of "aws s3 ls", which may include
    non-object lines or hide entries; not examined).
Q5. Is the loss reproducible? One run only. A repeat (with the instrumentation of Q1) is planned.

LIMITS
------
L1. One run, one day. A repeat of S1 and the scenario S2 (two nodes stopped) are open.
L2. The stop was done by hand in Proxmox; the script brackets the failure by the probe (last answer to first silence: 09:57:12 to 09:57:15).
L3. The recovery watch counts files of the volumes of the whole bucket (seed set and earlier runs included), so only differences between nodes and over time are meaningful, and the rule "bytes stable"
    did not fire because the probe kept writing (the watch lasted the full 30 minutes).
L4. The key list of the unreadable objects was read from the screen of the VM session; the counts (24 per endpoint) are from the console of the script.
L5. The result is for this configuration only (replication 002 on three nodes, filer with the embedded store on every node, S3 on every node, no load balancer). Another filer store or another replication
    setting is not tested.
L6. No comparison between systems is made in this record. For the RustFS runs see results/rustfs-vm-fault-tolerance-S1-repeat-2026-10-12.txt (there every acknowledged write was readable afterwards).
