PROVEN (read from the console, the ledger and the timeline)
-----------------------------------------------------------
P1. The loss of the first S1 is reproduced: in this run 23 writes were acknowledged by a surviving S3 endpoint and are not readable afterwards through any of the three endpoints (23 per endpoint).
    They are exactly the acknowledgements between 13:46:21 and 13:58:31, i.e. while ceph1 was stopped (the head_object test saw ceph1 fail at 13:45:34, 47 s before the first lost
    acknowledgement; in the first S1 the first lost acknowledgement was 29 s after the head_object failure). Acknowledgements before (181, 13:45:30) and from 13:59:05 (206) are readable.
P2. The file counts of ceph0 and ceph2 did not grow from 13:47:56 to 13:56:47 (9 minutes) although 17 writes were acknowledged (183 to 200 counted by the timeline): acknowledged, but no stored file
    (a read of the counts, not a proof of the mechanism). Same picture as in the first S1.
P3. While ceph1 was down, the master of ceph0 listed only two data nodes and no writable volume for any layout (13:50:56). With replication 002 (three copies) it could assign no volume for a new
    write. All 16 diagnostic writes by curl in that time got no answer within 30 s and nothing was stored (the read back failed, the assign answer was empty, writable volumes 0). From the first
    write after the return (13:59:09, 10.4 s) all 84 writes were stored and readable through two endpoints, with 5 writable volumes.
P4. After the return of ceph1 the lag stays: ceph1 42 files behind ceph0 and ceph2 5 files behind, constant for 30 minutes; no repair of the missed writes was seen. The lag grew from 28 to 42
    during this outage; in the earlier runs it was 19 for ceph1 (first S1, 10:12 to 11:33), 28 for ceph1 and 5 for ceph2 after S2 (12:01 to 13:39). So over the day it only grew in the
    outages and never shrank at the sample times of the records (10:12 to 14:29).
P5. The same client behaves differently in the same minutes: the probe (aws s3 cp, 1 MiB objects) got an ACKNOWLEDGEMENT for each round that did not go to ceph1, while ft-diag.sh (curl) got no
    answer at all within 30 s, and one test with aws s3api put-object ended after 26 s without a stored object. So the acknowledgement of the probe cannot be a stored write, and an S3 PUT through
    another client or another command did not get any acknowledgement.

NOT PROVEN, open questions (not to be written as findings)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Q1. Why does "aws s3 cp" get a success answer for a write that cannot be stored, while curl and "aws s3api put-object" get none? Candidates: a different request (for example the header
    Expect: 100-continue, the checksum or the payload signing mode) that makes the S3 gateway answer before the chunk is written; a different path in the gateway. Settles: in an outage capture
    the full HTTP exchange of one "aws s3 cp --debug" write (status line and headers) and of one curl write with and without the Expect header; tcpdump on port 8333 of ceph0 (tcpdump is installed
    on ceph0); and the log of the containers started with a log level that writes (the logs were empty in all runs). Also read the SeaweedFS source of the S3 PUT path when no volume can be assigned.
Q2. Was the acknowledgement of the probe really an HTTP 200 of SeaweedFS or a client side success without an answer from the server? Not captured (the probe records only the exit code of the
    aws cli). Settles: same capture as Q1.
Q3. Why did the loss start about 50 s after the stop and not at the stop? Candidates: the master lists ceph1 for some seconds until its heartbeat times out. Settles: master /dir/status and
    /cluster/status with time stamps right after a stop.
Q4. Will the lag of ceph1 (42 files) and of ceph2 (5 files) be repaired by a vacuum or by "weed shell volume.fix.replication"? Not tested.
Q5. Writes were storable again from 13:59:05/13:59:09. The start command finished at 13:59:36 and ssh answered at 13:59:15. Which component was back when the first write succeeded (the volume
    server of ceph1 may have started by itself) is not known. Same open point as Q2 of the first S1 (writes storable from 10:10:09 while the script's status port row of ceph1 was still DOWN
    until 10:10:54): both runs are consistent with "writable again once ceph1 is back", the mechanism is not shown.

LIMITS
------
L1. Two runs of S1 and one of S2 on the same cluster without a clean up; the lag of ceph1 and ceph2 from the earlier runs is the starting condition.
L2. The user stopped ceph1 about 67 s after the signal and the diagnostic was started at 13:46:21 (45 s after the script saw the silence), so the first 51 s after the stop have no diagnostic
    rows. The power on was done after the signal at 13:57:36; the exact click time is not known.
L3. The diagnostic uses curl and one test used the aws cli s3api; neither equals the probe command (aws s3 cp). Only the probe produced acknowledgements in the outage.
L4. The diagnostic writes (84 objects after the return, 16 failed attempts during) are in the same bucket and in the file counts of the recovery watch (the counts of the watch include them).
L5. The key list of the unreadable objects (ceph0 endpoint) was read from the screen of the VM session; the counts per endpoint are from the console. Rows of the diagnostic are partly read from the
    screen (rows 1 to 17 and 41 to 44; the counts per column pattern are from an awk summary of the whole file).
L6. No comparison between systems is made in this record. For RustFS see results/rustfs-vm-fault-tolerance-S1-repeat-2026-10-12.txt (there every acknowledged write was readable afterwards).
