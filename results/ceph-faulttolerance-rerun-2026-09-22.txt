Ceph fault tolerance rerun — measured recovery time and continuous availability
================================================================================
Date:        21-22 September 2026 (times are CEST, as recorded on ceph0)
Cluster:     Ceph Tentacle 20.2.4, 3 VMs (debian/ceph0, ceph1, ceph2), one OSD per host
Node killed: ceph1, hard stop via Proxmox (not a graceful shutdown)
Purpose:     Repeat of the 18-21 September test to MEASURE the two things that test
             only inferred: how long recovery actually takes, and whether S3 stayed
             available throughout. See setup_notes.md for why the first run's numbers
             were withdrawn.

METHOD
------
Availability probe (running continuously on ceph0 for the whole test, writing and
reading a small object through the local RGW every ~5 s and logging the outcome):

    # /root/availlog.sh
    export AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID=thesiskey
    export AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY=<CEPH_TEST_SECRET_KEY>
    E=http://localhost:80
    echo probe > /tmp/probe.bin
    while true; do
      T=$(date +%H:%M:%S)
      aws --endpoint-url $E s3 cp /tmp/probe.bin s3://thesis-test/probe.bin >/dev/null 2>&1 && W=WRITE_OK || W=WRITE_FAIL
      aws --endpoint-url $E s3 cp s3://thesis-test/probe.bin /tmp/back.bin >/dev/null 2>&1 && R=READ_OK || R=READ_FAIL
      echo "$T $W $R"
      sleep 5
    done

    nohup bash /root/availlog.sh > /root/avail.log 2>&1 &

Recovery poll (started after the node was restarted, exits on HEALTH_OK):

    while true; do T=$(date +%H:%M:%S); H=$(cephadm shell -- ceph health 2>/dev/null); \
      echo "$T $H" >> /root/events.log; echo "$T $H"; \
      case "$H" in HEALTH_OK*) break;; esac; sleep 20; done

MEASURED RESULTS
----------------
Outage duration (node down):          13 min 45 s   (23:01:55 → 23:15:40)
Time to full HEALTH_OK:               10 min 24 s   (23:15:40 → 23:26:04)
Time to data redundancy restored:     under 4 min 22 s  (23:15:40 → by 23:20:02)
Availability probes:                  268 samples, ~5 s apart, 23:00:07 → 23:27:14
Probe failures:                       0   (`grep -c FAIL /root/avail.log` → 0)

Every write and every read succeeded, without exception, across the entire test:
before the failure, throughout the 13 min 45 s outage, during recovery, and after.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION — two different "recovery times"
------------------------------------------------------
The recovery poll waits for HEALTH_OK, but HEALTH_OK is gated by `1 hosts fail
cephadm check`, which is the orchestrator's own management-layer check reconnecting
to the returned host. It is NOT a measure of data recovery. The degraded-data
warning had already cleared by the first poll at 23:20:02.

So the cluster's DATA was healthy again in under 4 min 22 s, while the
ORCHESTRATOR took 10 min 24 s to declare HEALTH_OK. The Replication chapter should
quote the data figure and explain the difference, since quoting 10 min 24 s alone
would overstate how long the cluster was actually at reduced redundancy.

This also explains the ~11-12 minute figure recorded (and later withdrawn as
unmeasured) in the 18-21 September run: that number was the orchestrator check
clearing, read indirectly off `ceph -s` counters, not the data recovering.

KNOWN LIMITATION OF THIS RUN
----------------------------
The exact moment data redundancy was restored was NOT captured. A mangled paste of
the polling loop into the Proxmox browser console cost the first ~4.5 minutes after
restart, so the first successful poll was at 23:20:02, by which point recovery had
already completed. The figure is therefore an upper bound (under 4 min 22 s), not a
measurement. To capture it exactly, the poll must record PG state (`ceph pg stat` or
the `pgs:` line of `ceph -s`) from t=0 rather than only `ceph health`, and must be
started before the node is restarted.
