PROVEN (read from the console, the ledger and the timeline)
-----------------------------------------------------------
P1. With two of three nodes down, a read of objects written earlier (430 probe objects) through the one surviving endpoint fails, while the 45 seed objects can be read (475 objects read, 45 OK). After the nodes were
    back, all of these probe objects are readable except the 16 of P2. So reading recent objects needed the other nodes (the reason is not known, see Q1).
P2. 16 writes were acknowledged by an S3 endpoint and are not readable afterwards through any of the three endpoints (verify result ERROR_OR_EMPTY; the HTTP code was read for S1 only): 11 of them were
    acknowledged by ceph0 while it was the only node up, and 5 were acknowledged in the 14 s before the first node was seen silent. The file count on ceph0 did not grow during the single node phase although
    8 writes were acknowledged (431 to 439).
P3. After the return of the two nodes, ceph1 stayed 28 files and ceph2 5 files behind ceph0 for 30 minutes; there was no repair. The lag of ceph1 from S1 (19 files) was still present 80 minutes after that restart.
P4. A client that is sent to a stopped node waits for its time out. The longest gap between acknowledged writes was 71.6 s (probe round with two stopped endpoints), and 46 attempts failed.
P5. The seed objects (written long before the test) stayed readable through the one surviving node and after the recovery.

NOT PROVEN, open questions (not to be written as findings)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Q1. Why can ceph0 alone not read the recent probe objects but can read the seed objects? Candidates: the master needs a quorum (two of three masters) to look up volume locations, and the filer caches the locations of old
    volumes; or the filer entries of recent objects are on other nodes. Settles: the same read test with curl against the filer and the volume server of ceph0 and the master status during an outage.
Q2. Why were 5 writes acknowledged in the 14 s before the first silence lost, although all three nodes answered? The stop was done by hand in Proxmox and the first silence in the probe is 11:47:23; the stop may have
    started earlier than the probe saw it, but 14 s is long for that. Possible link with Q1 of the S1 record (acknowledged but not stored). Settles: the instrumented repeat (scripts/ft-diag.sh) and a repeat of S2.
Q3. Same open question as in the S1 record: why are writes acknowledged with fewer than three nodes (replication 002) although no file is stored (the file count of ceph0 stays constant)?
Q4. Would the 28 and 5 missing files be repaired later by a vacuum or by "weed shell volume.fix.replication"? Not tested.
