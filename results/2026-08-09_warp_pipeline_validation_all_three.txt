WHY THESE ARE NOT DATA
  - single run per system, not repeated (protocol requires >= 5)
  - short measured window (~17 s after Warp trims ramp-up/ramp-down)
  - no warm-up period
  - uncontrolled host state (laptop, other processes not accounted for)
  - deliberately low concurrency (4), chosen for diagnosability not realism
  - the three systems are NOT equivalently configured: Garage runs at
    replication_factor = 1, SeaweedFS and RustFS are plain single-node defaults.
    They are not comparable as deployed.

OBSERVATIONS
================================================================================

1. RESULT: zero errors on all three. Warp drives every lightweight system in the
   study. The measurement chain is validated for SeaweedFS, RustFS and Garage.

2. SEAWEEDFS AND RUSTFS ARE INDISTINGUISHABLE HERE. 94.52 vs 91.63 MiB/s is well
   inside the noise of runs this unstable. No claim should be made that either is
   faster on this evidence.

3. GARAGE DIFFERS ON EVERY AXIS, not only throughput: under half the throughput,
   roughly 2.5x the median latency, and a p99 of 706 ms against 117-197 ms.

   The TTFB figure is the informative one. A median TTFB of 78 ms against 25-29 ms
   means the delay occurs BEFORE data transfer begins, which points at per-request
   overhead rather than bandwidth.

   HYPOTHESIS, NOT A FINDING: at 1 MiB this is a metadata-heavy workload, and
   Garage's architecture (LMDB metadata store, 256 partitions, quorum machinery
   present even at replication_factor = 1) plausibly costs more per object. The
   test that would settle it is an object-size sweep: if the cause is per-request
   overhead, the gap should narrow substantially at 100 MiB and 1 GiB. It must be
   verified before being stated in the thesis.

4. GARAGE'S VARIANCE IS THE MOST STRIKING NUMBER. Its slowest one-second window
   managed 2.2 MiB/s against a 45.9 MiB/s median - a 23x intra-run spread, where
   SeaweedFS and RustFS showed 3.4x and 5.0x. That is not ordinary noise; it is a
   stall. A periodic metadata flush or fsync would be consistent with a system
   designed to prioritise durability on unreliable nodes, but this is unverified.

5. CONSEQUENCE FOR TEST DESIGN. If a system stalls periodically, run LENGTH
   matters as much as repetition COUNT: a 17-second window may miss a stall
   entirely or land squarely on one, and neither is representative. This is
   concrete support for the proposal to lengthen individual runs rather than
   relying only on repeating short ones - a question already open with the
   supervisor.

6. VARIANCE ACROSS ALL THREE reinforces the >= 5 repetitions rule. Even the most
   stable system here (SeaweedFS, 3.4x spread) could report substantially
   different averages between two consecutive short runs.
