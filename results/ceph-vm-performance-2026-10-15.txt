GC (what was proven about the stop of attempt 1 and about the fix)
------------------------------------------------------------------
G1. In attempt 1 the gc queue held 8430 entries and about 33 GiB were pending while the OSDs were at 58 percent used (read from the cluster during the stop; the value 33 GiB as printed then).
G2. In the second series the space guard waited twice: at 64 percent and at 77 percent used (highest OSD), each time the first try of the wait loop (1 of 20) freed the space after the gc run and the series went on.
    The true peak between two checks is unknown (77 percent is the highest value seen).
G3. A claim about the cause of the slow freeing beyond "objects wait in the gc queue and gc process frees them" (for example the effect of rgw_gc_obj_min_wait) is NOT tested.

PROVEN (read from the tables, arithmetic mine)
----------------------------------------------
P1. Every recorded run finished without errors. The three runs of a configuration lie close together for most configurations. Largest spreads (maximum minus minimum, as percent of the minimum):
    GET 1 MiB at concurrency 1, 145.85 to 163.08 MiB/s (11.8 percent), PUT 1 MiB at concurrency 1, 33.97 to 35.75 (5.2 percent), PUT 16 MiB, 288.59 to 301.58 (4.5 percent); all other
    configurations 3.3 percent or less.
P2. Latency and throughput agree with the concurrency (Little's law, concurrency divided by average latency): PUT 1 MiB concurrency 1: 1 MiB / 29.3, 29.4, 28.0 ms = 34.1, 34.0, 35.7 MiB/s
    against 34.16, 33.97, 35.75; PUT 1 MiB concurrency 64: 64 / 225.4, 228.9, 222.3 ms = 284, 280, 288 MiB/s against 277.55, 275.84, 277.35 (2 to 4 percent above).
P3. PUT throughput grows with concurrency from 34 MiB/s (1 user) to 256 (16 users) and 277 (64 users): between 16 and 64 users it grows by 8 percent only. GET grows to 1209 at 16 users and is lower at 64 (1120).
P4. Drive writes against client volume (sum over the three nodes of wr_MiBs times the samples of the Warp window, divided by client MiB/s times 60 s; mean of the three runs; the window is the
    whole Warp process and longer than the measured minute, so the ratio is up to a few percent off): 3.03 to 3.08 for every PUT configuration with objects of 1 MiB or more (3 copies would give 3.0),
    7.81 for 50 KiB. For GET the drives read 0.41 to 0.47 of the client volume at 1 MiB, 0.75 at 16 and 100 MiB, 0.01 at 50 KiB.

    highest CPU in every configuration except GET 50 KiB (PUT 50 KiB: 67 against 64 and 63).

OPEN (observed, not explained)
------------------------------
Q1. Why the drives read only 0.41 to 0.75 of the bytes the client received in the GET runs (and 0.01 at 50 KiB). A cache hit in the page cache of the nodes or in the BlueStore cache is a HYPOTHESIS (test:
    read the OSD cache and the page cache counters during a GET run); the page cache was dropped before each GET run, but the data set is read repeatedly within the run.
Q2. Why PUT 50 KiB writes 7.8 times the client volume to the drives. The September record results/ceph-smallobject-writeamplification-2026-09-24.txt examined this on the earlier cluster; whether the
    same cause (BlueStore deferred writes, three copies) holds here is a HYPOTHESIS, not tested on this series.
Q3. Whether the PUT throughput at 90 percent CPU of ceph2 (16 MiB, 100 MiB, 64 users) is limited by the load node. HYPOTHESIS, untested; the test would need Warp on a host that is not a storage node, which is not
    available on the three VMs (limit L1).

Limits
------
L1. ceph2 is both load generator and storage node (see P5).
L2. The space guard and gc: see section GC, the peak space use between two checks is not known.
L3. The page cache drop before each GET run is set, not logged per run.
