NOT PROVEN, hypotheses and open points (not to be written as findings)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
O1. How ceph1 got the objects back (hypotheses: RustFS restores missing shards by itself when a node returns, at start or by a background scan). Not examined: the container
    log has 0 lines with "heal" at the default log level, so it neither confirms nor excludes it. Settles: the RustFS source (heal on start or scanner) or a rerun with a higher
    log level on ceph1.
O2. The exact moment the repair began and ended is bracketed, not measured: the first sample after the restart (21:04:57) already shows ceph1 at 269 and the next (21:05:18)
    at 301; the nodes are sampled one after the other by ssh, so the rows of one sample are a few seconds apart. The repair of about 105 objects (about 105 MiB of object data,
    about 210 MiB raw over six drives with 3+3 coding, my arithmetic) therefore took at most about 26 s, at light load.
O3. Content of the restored files is not compared shard by shard (no per shard hash); counts, file counts and bytes are equal on all drives and the read back through
    192.168.1.71 gave OK 1232, which reads through one node but can be served by reconstruction from the other shards.
O4. Whether the repair time depends on the amount missed (this run: about 105 objects, 12 minutes) and on the load: one run, light load (one 1 MiB write every few seconds).
O5. The recovery watch stop rule of ft.sh ("bytes stable for 180 s") cannot fire while the probe writes; the watch ended by its 30 minute limit. A better rule (equal counts on
    all drives for N samples) is not implemented.
O6. Cause of the 25 s gap and of the failed attempts until 21:04:48 (hypothesis: a write in flight to the stopped node waits for a timeout; read timeout 25 s in the script).
O7. Planned restart (S3) not run. SeaweedFS, Garage and Ceph not run (same method later).
