       during the outage failed (ERROR_OR_EMPTY 207, no OK), and the fixed object could not be read through 192.168.1.72 from 16:19:37 to 16:28:17. Writes: not one
       write was acknowledged between 16:19:12 and 16:28:06; the 97 failed attempts equal 32 loops (index 163 to 194) times 3 endpoints plus the one failure at 16:28:15
       (my arithmetic, it matches the number printed by the script), so every attempt on every endpoint failed during the outage.
S2-P2. Together with S1 this brackets the behaviour on this setup at node granularity: with four of six drives up (S1) reads and writes continued; with two of six up
       (S2) neither worked. Three of six drives cannot be tested with whole nodes (a node holds two drives).
S2-P3. The service returned without any repair command after the nodes were back and RustFS was started on them: first acknowledged write at 16:28:06, 5 s after the
       start command finished on ceph2 and 6 s after ceph1 answered; the surviving node's own endpoint served reads again at 16:28:17.
S2-P4. No acknowledged write was lost: all 1110 objects (45 seed and 1065 acknowledged) are readable through every endpoint with the recorded sha256 after the recovery.
S2-P5. Nothing was acknowledged during the outage, so there were no writes to repair afterwards; S2 therefore says nothing about the repair of missed writes (see S1, O6).
S2-P6. After the reboot no Ceph container ran on ceph1 and ceph2 (container lists at about 17:25), consistent with the disabled cephadm target.

NOT PROVEN, hypotheses and open points (not to be written as findings)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
O1. Why two drives are not enough (hypothesis: reading needs at least the three data shards and writing needs a quorum of at least four of six drives, as usual for
    erasure coding 3+3). Not read in the RustFS documentation or source here. Settles: the RustFS documentation or source on read and write quorum.
O2. Why the first failed attempt comes 30 s after the last acknowledged write (16:19:12 and 16:19:42; S1 had a similar 26.8 s gap): hypothesis that a write in flight to a
    stopping node waits for a timeout. Settles: the ledger rows and the client timeouts (the script sets a read timeout of 25 s and a connect timeout of 3 s, from the code).
O3. The failure of ceph0's own endpoint at 16:28:15 (ServiceUnavailable) and its first successful read at 16:28:17, although ceph1 had answered since 16:28:00 and ceph2
    since 16:28:07 and an acknowledged write went through ceph2 at 16:28:06: cause unknown (hypothesis: the node re-establishes its connections to the peers after an
    interval). Settles: the RustFS log of ceph0 for that time (not read).
O4. The drive level check used in S1 is not informative here: the probe keys were reused (S2 overwrote probe/1 to about probe/1097, the objects of S1; see the CORRECTION of
    the S1 record). The counts read after S2 (about 17:30: 1179 entries, 2358 files and 412824929 bytes on each of the six drives) are the same as after S1 and show nothing
    about S2.
O5. The data size reading of the recovery watch is noisy, as in S1; it was not evaluated for S2.
O6. Planned restart (S3) and a repeat with unique probe keys and a per drive count (S1 repeat) are not run.

LIMITS
------
L1. Light load (one 1 MiB write per probe loop). One run, one day, one outage length (6 minutes), two nodes. No repetition and no statistics.
L2. The stop and the power on are done by a person in Proxmox; reaction time is inside the numbers (30 s between the signal and the silence of both endpoints). The user
    stopped ceph1 and ceph2 one after the other, not at the same instant.
L3. The read test during the outage went through ceph0 only (script design) and ran from about 16:20 to 16:24; the outage lasted from 16:19:18 to the power on.
L4. The script printed "D: unbound variable" once at its end (see L3 of the S1 record); the script at d3b23b8 ran; it was changed afterwards (key prefix per run).
L5. Redundancy differs between the systems (erasure coding 3+3 against three copies in the others): "two nodes down" leaves two of six shards here and one of three copies elsewhere.
