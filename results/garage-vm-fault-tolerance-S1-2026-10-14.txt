PROVEN
------
P1. With ceph1 down, writes and reads through ceph0 and ceph2 worked again after a stall of about one minute after the stop (the failure detection of Garage: pings every 15 s, the connection closed after five failed pings).
P2. During that stall requests through the surviving endpoints do not answer (curl 000 after 30 s; aws client time out after 25 s); the ledger has 32 failed attempts, all to ceph1.
P3. In both runs acknowledged writes (ledger, exit code 0) are not readable after the recovery: 2 in run 1, 1 in run 2 (about 0.1 percent of the acknowledged writes in run 2). In run 2 the object does not exist
    afterwards on any endpoint (404), and the ceph0 log shows its PUT request 25.1 s before the acknowledgement.
P4. The same stall of 192.168.1.72 caused the only two diagnostic writes that did not behave normally; the diagnostic write that was in the stall was not acknowledged (code 000) and is correctly absent.
P5. After the return of ceph1 the resync queue is empty and without errors; ceph1 data directories are not identical to those of ceph0 and ceph2 (+10 files, fewer bytes) in both runs.

NOT PROVEN, open (not to be written as findings)
-------------------------------------------------
Q1. Why was probe 169 (and in run 1 probes 214 and 215) acknowledged but not stored? All three acknowledged-but-missing writes were acknowledged about 25 s after their request started, which is also the read timeout
    of the client, so the time may be a coincidence or may be the point where Garage or the client gave up. The client check above rules out an aws exit code 0 after a plain timeout, but does not rule out
    other client behaviour. Settles: a probe that records the ETag and the elapsed time of every acknowledged PUT (aws s3api put-object, JSON answer) and heads the key right after any acknowledgement that took
    more than 10 s, in a repeat of S1.
Q2. Is the difference of ceph1 (+10 files in disk1, fewer bytes) a normal effect (for example block files in different states) or missing data? The 1213 objects are readable through every endpoint, so no object is
    missing by this test. Settles: a Garage repair/scrub listing, or a block list comparison between the nodes.
Q3. Is the write quorum 2 of 3? Written from memory in the predictions, not verified in the Garage documentation or by a test of the data directories.
Q4. The cause of the seed read errors (fixed/head.bin, seed/1MiB-1 to -5) during the outage: they come first in the read order, which fits the stall of about a minute, but it was not shown.

LIMITS
------
L1. Run 2 is one run; run 1 was longer than planned and had no recovery watch of the method.
L2. The probe timestamps are written when a probe round ends; with a stopped node a round takes up to 28 s, so the times of state changes are only exact to that order.
L3. Numbers were read from the console of the VM session; where only the reply text of the session was read this is stated. The raw archives stay on ceph0 and have not been copied to this repository.
L4. The write diagnostic ran for 80 samples through two endpoints at 5 s; it saw one stall of about one minute, so it does not measure the rate of lost writes.
L5. Garage version 1.0.0 and the default settings of fsync, consistency mode and block compression were not changed and have not been looked up (see Q3).
L6. No comparison between systems is made in this record.
